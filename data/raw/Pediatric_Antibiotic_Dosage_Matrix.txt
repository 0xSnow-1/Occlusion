## 1. Weight-Based Treatment Dosing — Odontogenic Infection (Pediatric, Immunocompetent)

| Drug | Pediatric Dose (mg/kg/day) | Max Single Dose | Max Daily Dose | Frequency | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 50 mg/kg/day, divided | 500 mg | up to 2 g/day | TID | 3–7 days |
| Amoxicillin/Clavulanate | 45 mg/kg/day (amoxicillin component) | 500 mg | up to 1.75 g/day | BID–TID | 3–7 days |
| Penicillin V potassium | 25–50 mg/kg/day, divided | 500 mg | up to 2 g/day | QID | 3–7 days |
| Cephalexin | 25–50 mg/kg/day, divided | 500 mg | up to 2 g/day | QID | 3–7 days |
| Clindamycin | 20–30 mg/kg/day, divided | 300 mg | up to 1.8 g/day | TID–QID | 3–7 days |
| Azithromycin | 10 mg/kg day 1, then 5 mg/kg | 500 mg day 1, then 250 mg | — | Once daily | 5 days total |
| Metronidazole (adjunct only) | 20–30 mg/kg/day, divided | 500 mg | up to 4 g/day (adult ceiling) | TID | 3–7 days |

## 2. Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis — Pediatric Single-Dose Regimens (30–60 min pre-procedure)

| Situation | Agent | Pediatric Dose | Absolute Max (adult dose) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard, able to take oral meds | Amoxicillin | 50 mg/kg | 2 g |
| Unable to take oral meds | Ampicillin | 50 mg/kg IM/IV | 2 g |
| Unable to take oral meds | Cefazolin or Ceftriaxone | 50 mg/kg IM/IV | 1 g |
| Penicillin-allergic, oral | Cephalexin | 50 mg/kg | 2 g |
| Penicillin-allergic, oral | Azithromycin / Clarithromycin | 15 mg/kg | 500 mg |
| Penicillin-allergic, oral | Doxycycline (≥45 kg: adult dose; <45 kg: weight-based) | 2.2 mg/kg | 100 mg |
| Penicillin-allergic + unable to take oral | Cefazolin or Ceftriaxone | 50 mg/kg IM/IV | 1 g |

*Note: Clindamycin is **no longer recommended** by the 2021 AHA Scientific Statement as a penicillin-allergy alternative for IE prophylaxis, due to a higher rate of adverse reactions including C. difficile infection. It remains listed in Table 1 above only for treatment of active infection, not prophylaxis.*

## 3. Penicillin Allergy Classification & Antibiotic Selection Guide

| Allergy Severity | Clinical History | Safe to Use Cephalosporins? | Recommended Alternative Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-severe (Type IV / delayed) | Isolated mild rash, no urticaria/anaphylaxis, occurred >72h after dose | Generally yes (low cross-reactivity, <2%) | Cephalexin acceptable |
| Severe (Type I / IgE-mediated) | Urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm within minutes-hours | **No** — avoid all beta-lactams including cephalosporins | Azithromycin, clarithromycin, clindamycin, or doxycycline |
| Uncertain / undocumented | Family-reported "penicillin allergy" with no clinical detail available | Treat as severe until clarified | Refer for allergy testing if recurring need; use non-beta-lactam in interim |

## 4. Quick-Reference: "50 mg/kg up to 2 g" Rule

For amoxicillin, cephalexin, and penicillin V in children:

```
Dose (mg) = 50 mg/kg x patient weight (kg)
Ceiling   = 2000 mg (2 g), regardless of calculated value above this threshold
```

Example: A 30 kg child → 50 × 30 = 1500 mg → below the 2 g ceiling → administer 1500 mg (1.5 g).
Example: A 55 kg adolescent → 50 × 55 = 2750 mg → exceeds ceiling → cap dose at 2000 mg (2 g), do not exceed adult dose.
